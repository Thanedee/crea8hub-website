How much does social media management cost in Nigeria? 2026 prices

What Nigerian businesses pay a freelancer, an in-house hire or an agency to run their social media, what pushes the price up, and the costs that sit on top of the monthly fee.

By the Crea8Hub team16 November 20268 min read

The short answer

  • FreelancerUsually the cheapest option. Many charge each client tens of thousands to a few hundred thousand naira a month, but one person can only do so much.
  • In-house₦150,000–₦350,000 a month in base salary for a social media manager with 2–4 years' experience, before tools, devices and content production.
  • AgencyA team covering strategy, content, video, community management and reporting. Crea8Hub's retainers start at ₦500,000 a month.
  • Ad spendAlmost always paid on top. Meta ads in Nigeria average about ₦2,100 per 1,000 impressions.
  • ARCONSocial media adverts need approval before they run, so budget for vetting fees and time.

"How much do you charge?" is the first question most businesses ask about social media management, and the honest answer is that it depends on who does the work and how much of it there is. A freelancer posting three times a week on Instagram and a team running content, video, ads and customer replies across four platforms are both called "social media management". They cost very different amounts.

This guide breaks down what each option costs in Nigeria in 2026, using published salary and advertising data, plus our own prices as a Lagos agency. Sources are listed at the bottom.

Salaries rise with experience. Profolio puts a senior social media manager (4–6 years) at ₦300,000–₦500,000 a month and a head of social or digital marketing manager at ₦400,000–₦700,000. Banks, telecoms and large FMCG companies tend to pay at the upper end.

What it means

Compare like with like. A freelancer's fee buys one person's time. A salary buys one person full-time, plus everything they need to do the job. An agency fee buys a team. The right choice depends on how much content you need and how much of your sales happen on social media.

02 · The priceWhat makes social media management cost more

Whoever you hire, these are the things that move the price.

  1. Number of platforms. Each platform needs content made for it. A TikTok video, a LinkedIn post and an Instagram carousel are three different jobs, not one post copied three times.
  2. Posts and videos per month. Video is usually the biggest cost. Short-form video needs planning, filming, editing and often presenters or models.
  3. Community management. Replying to comments and DMs daily costs more than checking in weekly. For many Nigerian businesses, sales start in the DMs, so slow replies cost money.
  4. Paid ads management. Planning, running and optimising ads is a separate skill from posting content. It's often priced separately.
  5. Strategy and reporting. A monthly report that tells you what worked, and what changes next, takes time to produce. It's also how you know the money is working.
  6. Your industry. Finance, health and other regulated sectors need more careful copy, more checks and more lead time for approvals.

03 · On topThe costs on top of the monthly fee

The management fee is rarely the whole bill. Ask every freelancer or agency which of these are included.

Ad spend

Money paid to Meta, TikTok or Google for ads usually sits outside the management fee. Nigeria is one of the cheaper markets to advertise in. Grey's 2026 guide puts the average cost per 1,000 impressions (CPM) on Facebook at about $1.50, or roughly ₦2,100, with clicks typically costing ₦30–₦300 and leads ₦500–₦5,000 or more, depending on your objective and industry.

At that average, a ₦200,000 monthly budget would buy roughly 95,000 impressions. Real results vary a lot with targeting, creative and season, so treat it as a starting point.

ARCON approval

Advertising on social media needs approval from the Advertising Regulatory Council of Nigeria (ARCON) before it runs. A Federal High Court ruling in April 2025 confirmed ARCON's power to regulate adverts on social media. In 2023, ARCON published vetting fees of ₦7,500 for the standard seven-day route, ₦70,000 for two days and ₦100,000 for one day. Check current fees before you budget, as they may have changed.

Production and creators

Photo and video shoots, models, locations and influencer fees are usually quoted per project. If your plan relies on regular video, find out whether filming is part of the retainer or charged separately.

Tools

Scheduling, design and analytics tools are often billed in dollars. Ask whether they're included or passed on to you.

04 · ValueWhy the cheapest option can cost more

A low monthly fee looks good until you count what's missing. The usual problems with a too-cheap arrangement are posting that stops when the person is busy, stock images that look like everyone else's, no plan behind the content, slow replies to customers, and no report showing whether any of it worked.

The most expensive mistake is losing access. If someone else created your page, ad account or business manager, you may not own it. Whoever you hire, make sure your business owns every account, with them added as a partner.

Questions to ask before you hire anyone

Who will actually work on my account? How many posts and videos will I get each month, and how many rounds of changes? Is community management included, and how fast will you reply? Is ad management included, and is ad spend separate? Who handles ARCON approval? What will you report each month? Do we own the accounts, ad accounts and content files?

05 · The choiceWhen you need an agency, and when you don't

You may not need an agency yet if you're just starting, your budget is small, and one platform is enough. A good freelancer, or the owner posting to a clear plan, can work well at this stage.

An agency usually makes sense when:

  • A real share of your sales comes through social media and DMs, so slow replies or missed posts cost you money.
  • You need regular video, not just graphics.
  • You're running paid ads and want them managed alongside your content.
  • You're in a regulated industry such as finance, where copy and approvals need extra care.
  • You need consistency that doesn't depend on one person staying.

QuestionsGood to know

How much does social media management cost in Nigeria?

It depends on who does the work. Freelancers usually charge each client tens of thousands to a few hundred thousand naira a month. An in-house social media manager with 2–4 years' experience earns about ₦150,000–₦350,000 a month in base salary, before tools and production costs. Agency retainers cost more because you get a team: Crea8Hub's start at ₦500,000 a month. Ad spend and ARCON fees are usually extra.

How much does a social media manager earn in Nigeria per month?

The average range is ₦120,000–₦300,000 a month, with a median of about ₦180,000, according to Profolio's 2026 salary guide. Senior social media managers earn about ₦300,000–₦500,000, and heads of social or digital about ₦400,000–₦700,000.

Is ad spend included in social media management fees?

Usually not. The management fee pays for the work, while ad spend is paid to Meta, TikTok or Google. In Nigeria, Meta ads average about ₦2,100 per 1,000 impressions (Grey, 2026). Always ask whether ad spend, ad management and production are included in a quote.

Is it cheaper to hire in-house or use an agency?

A single in-house hire often has a lower monthly cost than an agency, but one person rarely covers strategy, design, video, copywriting, ads and community management equally well. Once you add tools, devices, shoots and management time, an agency can be better value if you need several of those skills.

Do social media adverts in Nigeria need ARCON approval?

Yes. A Federal High Court ruling in April 2025 confirmed that ARCON can regulate advertising on social media. In 2023 ARCON published vetting fees from ₦7,500 for the standard seven-day route to ₦100,000 for one-day approval. See our ARCON approval guide.
