      02How we calculate

      The maths behind it

      We start with your total budget, take off VAT and any card fees, convert the rest to dollars, then apply typical Nigerian costs for each platform.

      01

      Budget to ad spend

      Your total includes 7.5% VAT, which Meta and Google add to Nigerian ad bills. If you pay in dollars, we also take off your card or FX fee. What's left is what the platforms can spend.

      02

      Spend to impressions

      Ad spend divided by cost per 1,000 impressions (CPM) gives impressions. For Google Search we use cost per click instead. Reach is impressions divided by 2.5, a typical frequency.

      03

      Clicks to results

      Clicks come from impressions and click-through rate. Leads and sales come from clicks and a conversion rate. Awareness goals buy cheaper impressions. Lead and sales goals cost more per impression.

      03Tips

      Five ways to stretch your naira

      01

      Pay in naira where you can

      Meta lets Nigerian ad accounts pay in naira by bank transfer, USSD or card. That avoids dollar card limits and FX mark-ups. You choose the currency when you create the ad account and can't change it later, so set it up right first time.

      02

      Test three to five creatives before you scale

      Spend the first 20% of your budget testing different images, videos and headlines. Then move the rest to the one or two that bring the cheapest results. A strong creative often halves your cost per result.

      03

      Use click-to-WhatsApp ads

      Many Nigerian customers would rather chat than fill in a form. Ads that open a WhatsApp chat usually cost less per lead, and your sales team can answer questions straight away. Reply fast, or the lead goes cold.

      04

      Get ARCON approval before you launch

      Most adverts aimed at Nigerians, including social media ads, need approval from the Advertising Regulatory Council of Nigeria first. Build the time and fee into your plan. Our ARCON approval guide explains the steps.

      05

      Time your spend around big moments

      Costs rise when every brand is advertising, for example around Black Friday and Detty December. Start earlier, build your audience while it's cheaper, then push harder on the days that matter. See our Nigeria campaign calendar for key dates.

      04Questions

      Ad budget FAQs

      What's the smallest budget worth running?

      You can start on Meta from about ₦2,000 a day, but you'll learn very little. We suggest at least ₦100,000 for a two-week test on one platform. TikTok asks for at least $20 a day per ad group, so it needs a bigger budget.

      Do I really pay VAT on Facebook and Google ads?

      Yes. Meta has added 7.5% VAT to ads bought by Nigerian advertisers since January 2022, and Google since April 2022. The Nigeria Tax Act 2025 kept the rate at 7.5%. If your business is VAT-registered, add your VAT number to your ad account so it shows on your receipts.

      Why is my exchange rate different from the one here?

      We use the official market rate. Banks, cards and fintech apps often charge more, and some add a fee on top. Type your own rate into the calculator, or raise the card or FX fee, to see the difference.

      Which platform should I start with?

      For most Nigerian small businesses, Facebook and Instagram with click-to-WhatsApp ads are the cheapest way to get leads. Add Google Search when people already search for what you sell. TikTok and YouTube work well for awareness with younger audiences.

      Will I get the results shown?

      Not necessarily. These are estimates based on average costs. Your offer, creative, targeting, season and how fast you reply all change the outcome. Use the range to plan, then check your real numbers after the first week. Read the method for details.
