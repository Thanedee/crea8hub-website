03Good to Know

Micro or macro: choosing the right influencers

Macro-influencers and celebrities give you huge reach quickly, which suits launches and big announcements. Micro-influencers, usually with a few thousand to around a hundred thousand followers, often have closer relationships with their audiences, higher engagement and lower fees, which suits sustained campaigns and niche products. Most effective campaigns in Nigeria combine both: a few big voices for attention and many smaller ones for trust and repetition. Platform choice matters too. TikTok and Instagram dominate for lifestyle, beauty, food and fashion; X works for conversation-driven and news-led campaigns; YouTube suits longer reviews and tutorials; and LinkedIn creators reach professionals and business decision-makers.

06Questions We Get Asked

Influencer Marketing FAQs

How much do influencers charge in Nigeria?

Influencer fees in Nigeria vary widely depending on follower count, engagement, platform, the type of content and how long you can use it. Micro-influencers cost a fraction of what celebrities charge. We'll recommend a mix of creators that fits your budget and goals.

How do you check that an influencer's followers are real?

We review engagement rates, comment quality, follower growth patterns and audience location, and ask creators for their own platform insights before recommending them.

Which platforms do you run influencer campaigns on?

Mainly Instagram, TikTok, X, YouTube and Facebook, and sometimes LinkedIn for B2B and professional audiences.

Do we get to approve the content before it's posted?

Yes. Creators share drafts for your approval, and the brief sets out what must be included and avoided, so content stays on-brand and compliant.

Do influencer posts need ARCON approval?

Paid influencer posts are a form of advertising in Nigeria, and ARCON's rules on advertising apply. We build compliance checks and clear sponsorship disclosure into every campaign.

What's the difference between an influencer and a UGC creator?

An influencer posts about your brand to their own followers, so you're paying for their reach and credibility. A UGC (user-generated content) creator makes authentic-looking videos and photos that you post on your own pages or use in ads, so you're paying for the content itself. Many campaigns use both.
